The best app idea you've ever heard will never get built. And the reason why explains why worse apps keep winning.
Think about that one friend who always has an app idea.
He pitched it at dinner before the food even showed up, and everyone at the table told him the same thing. You should build that.
Three years later, it's still a note on his phone. And nobody can really explain why, because the idea didn't get worse, and he never stopped talking about it.
What went wrong is that ideas and apps play by opposite rules.
An idea gets better the longer you think about it. So he kept thinking. He priced a developer. Forty thousand dollars. He started a coding course. He quit at lesson four.
So he learned that building is the most expensive step there is.
Apps work the other way around. Nobody can know in advance whether people want it. The answer only begins to exist once a real person taps on it.
And that step stopped being expensive. Now you open Your Brand on your phone, type the idea like a text to a friend, and get a first version you can tap. No code.
So while he keeps polishing the perfect plan, someone with a far worse idea types it into their phone, ships something embarrassing, and fixes it one message at a time. Until the app gets so big that people start calling it luck.
